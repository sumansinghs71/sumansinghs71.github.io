Inference RoutingProduction failure pattern

◫One inference region failed. Traffic kept going there.

Multi-region inventory is not resilience if the router cannot eject an unhealthy model endpoint.

router→region A ✓→region B 503→region C ✓
Redundancy exists; failure isolation does not.
Signals in production
  • A fixed fraction of requests return 5xx/timeouts.
  • Other regions remain healthy and have spare capacity.
  • Removing one endpoint from the pool immediately improves success rate.
Failure boundary

The routing layer fails to translate per-region health into traffic decisions quickly enough.

01

Why it happens

Static round-robin spreads healthy traffic and unhealthy traffic equally. A resilient model gateway needs region-level health, bounded retries, endpoint ejection and enough headroom for the surviving regions.

02

Production-shaped example

A six-region inference pool loses one region after a provider-side change. Five regions are healthy, but roughly one-sixth of requests continue failing because the router keeps treating every endpoint as equal.

What fooled the team
“we are multi-region”health checks that prove only DNS/TCP reachabilityall regions running near saturation
First 5 minutes
  1. Break errors down by region/deployment rather than global averages.
  2. Eject the bad endpoint and verify remaining capacity before shifting traffic.
  3. Watch retry amplification and queue growth after the shift.
03

Controls

Prevent the failure, detect it early, then recover without making it worse.

PREVENT
  • Use active inference probes plus rolling failure/latency health per region.
  • Reserve failover headroom and enforce per-region circuit breakers.
  • Classify retryable failures and cap the retry budget.
  • Version routing configuration and use controlled re-entry after recovery.
DETECT
  • Dashboard success, latency, throttling and saturation by endpoint.
  • Compare router decisions with endpoint-health state.
  • Probe independently of user traffic so a quiet region can still be declared unhealthy.
RECOVER
  • Automatically eject after a bounded unhealthy threshold.
  • Shift only to regions with confirmed headroom.
  • Reintroduce with canary traffic, then ramp.
Prove it with failure injection
  • hard 503 in one region
  • slow region / tail-latency failure
  • region recovery and re-entry
  • failover while survivors are near capacity
Monitor
success by regionejection countcapacity headroomretry ratere-entry failures
PRODUCTION INVARIANTIf healthy serving capacity still exists, loss of one region must not become loss of the product.

Redundancy is inventory. Resilience is routing away from the broken copy.
