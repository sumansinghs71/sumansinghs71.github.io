Reliability & Runtime

🌐 One inference region failed. Why did the product stay up?

Multi-region deployment is not resilience unless unhealthy capacity can be detected and removed from service.

What it looks like

  • Only one model endpoint starts returning 5xx errors or timeouts.
  • Aggregate availability drops even though healthy capacity exists elsewhere.
  • Recovery depends on a human editing routing configuration.

Why this happens

Multiple endpoints provide redundancy, but resilience needs health-aware routing, endpoint ejection, capacity awareness and controlled recovery. Static round-robin can keep feeding a failed region even while other regions are healthy.

A simple real-world example

An AI gateway distributes traffic across several regions. One region develops a provider-side routing failure. The others are healthy, but a fixed share of requests still enters the failed region until someone removes it.

What teams usually get wrong

  • Assuming multi-region automatically means failover.
  • Retrying the same unhealthy endpoint.
  • Failing over without checking spare capacity.
  • Returning recovered capacity at full traffic immediately.

What I would put in production

  • Use active and passive endpoint health signals.
  • Add regional circuit breakers and automatic endpoint ejection.
  • Retry into a different healthy failure domain with a bounded retry budget.
  • Alert on minimum healthy capacity, not just endpoint count.
  • Ramp recovered regions back gradually.

What I would monitor

availability by region
endpoint ejection events
healthy spare capacity
cross-region retry rate
Takeaway: One region unavailable should not mean the product is unavailable.
