Production problem

⌁ The answer looks fine, but RAG retrieved the wrong evidence

Generation can hide retrieval problems for a while, which makes this one of the quieter production failures.

What it looks like

  • Answers sound plausible but cite weak or irrelevant sources.
  • Quality drops only for certain products, tenants or document types.
  • A model upgrade appears to fix the problem temporarily even though retrieval did not improve.

Why this happens

RAG is a pipeline. A document can be missing at ingestion, split badly, embedded poorly, filtered out, ranked too low, or pushed out of the final context. If you score only the final answer, a capable model may answer from prior knowledge and hide the retrieval regression. That creates a dangerous false sense of health.

A simple real-world example

A policy agent is asked about a fee exception introduced last week. The latest policy was ingested, but metadata incorrectly labels it as region=US instead of GLOBAL. The EU query filters it out. The model still produces a reasonable answer from the older policy, and the final-answer evaluator gives partial credit. The actual failure is the metadata filter, not the model.

What teams usually get wrong

  • Looking only at answer accuracy.
  • Increasing top-k when the right document is filtered out before ANN search.
  • Blaming embeddings before checking ingestion and metadata.
  • Assuming a citation is grounding without checking whether it supports the claim.

What I would put in production

  • Trace source IDs from ingestion through retrieval, reranking and context assembly.
  • Maintain retrieval-specific evals such as recall@k and citation support.
  • Slice metrics by tenant, source, filter, language and document type.
  • Track source freshness and index lag.
  • Save failed-query candidates so engineers can inspect what the system actually saw.

What I would monitor

retrieval recall@k
zero-result rate by filter
citation support rate
index freshness lag
reranker contribution
